Tobacco & nicotine

What it is

Tobacco and nicotine use includes cigarettes, vaping and smokeless tobacco. Nicotine is addictive, so cravings and withdrawal can make stopping hard, and many people make several attempts before they stop.

Common things that keep the habit going include stress, daily routines such as a coffee or a drive, being around other people who smoke or vape, and alcohol.

In the app

What the app helps with

  • Choose your path

    Set a quit date, quit today, reduce gradually on a step-down schedule, or explore first with lessons and Rescue only.

  • Rescue for cravings

    One tap from home, with no typing. It asks what is pulling at you, offers the tool that helped last time, such as two minutes of slow breathing, and keeps a person one tap away.

  • One mission a day

    A small task tied to your own top reason, and a daily check-in of about a minute.

  • Medication center

    A place to record the quit-medication plan you agreed with your clinician. Education and self-tracking only; the app never prescribes.

  • Progress

    Smoke-free days and money not spent on tobacco, which a slip never erases.

Slip recovery

If you go off plan: the recovery plan

The slip recovery screen opens with “A slip is information, not the end.” These are its steps.

  1. 1

    What happened?

    Pick one: a puff or two, one cigarette, more, or prefer not to say. The smoke-free days you already have stay on screen.

  2. 2

    What was going on? (optional)

    Stress, around smokers, alcohol or scrolling late.

  3. 3

    Continue from this moment

    The recommended next step keeps your plan. You can choose a new quit time instead.

  4. 4

    Save and start a 2-min Rescue

    Saving adds the slip to your record without taking anything away, then opens a short Rescue tool.

Nothing resets: the app adds a record of the slip and never lowers a count.

What it includes

The areas BreatheFree covers.

Every part of BreatheFree follows the same principles as the rest of Tether Health: private by design, no judgment, and a route to a real person.

  • Prevention

    Plain-language material on how dependence forms, aimed at people who have not started or have started recently.

  • Cessation support

    Day-to-day help through a quit attempt: a plan the person owns, craving tools, and check-ins that take under two minutes.

  • Relapse prevention

    Treating a slip as information rather than failure, keeping prior progress intact and helping the person restart the same day.

  • Exposure reduction

    Support for reducing exposure where stopping outright is not the immediate goal, without presenting reduction as a safe endpoint.
